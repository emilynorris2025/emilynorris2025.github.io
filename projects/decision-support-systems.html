PROJECT 01 · DECISION SCIENCE

Constraint-Based Decision Support System

A scenario-driven optimization model built in MiniZinc to support complex decisions involving capacity constraints, mandatory participants, relationship dynamics, conflicts, and competing priorities.

THE CHALLENGE

Complex decisions rarely have one perfect answer.

This project explores how constraint programming can support complex decision-making when multiple requirements, competing objectives, and stakeholder relationships must be considered simultaneously.

TECHNICAL APPROACH

How the optimization model works.

Decision Variables

Each participant is assigned a decision variable representing their placement at a table or their exclusion from the event.

A separate Boolean decision variable controls which high-level scenario the optimization model evaluates.

Hard Constraints

Hard constraints enforce requirements that cannot be violated, including table capacity, mandatory participants, reserved seating, and scenario-specific requirements.

Objective Function

The model converts qualitative preferences into a measurable optimization objective. Positive scores reward social cohesion, while significant penalties discourage incompatible pairings.

KEY ACHIEVEMENTS

What the system demonstrates.

01

Scenario-Aware Decisions

Designed the model to dynamically adjust downstream requirements based on alternative high-level decisions.

02

Complex Rule Integration

Combined physical capacity constraints, mandatory attendance, relationship preferences, and conflict rules into one optimization system.

03

Explainable Optimization

Built a transparent scoring framework that makes it possible to understand why one solution is preferred over another.

04

Automated Reporting

Structured the model output to report the selected scenario, objective score, conflict penalties, assignments, and excluded participants.

WHY IT MATTERS

From seating plans to real-world decision systems.

While this project uses a fictional event-planning scenario, the same optimization framework can be adapted to workforce scheduling, resource allocation, logistics, portfolio prioritization, supply chain decisions, and other complex problems involving competing constraints.
